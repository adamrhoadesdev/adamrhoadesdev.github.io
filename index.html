Featured Projects

Player Swing Simulation and Dynamic AI Dodging

Simulated the player's weapon path to predict precise AI contact points, then warned enemies to blend contextual dodge animations. The system made combat more dynamic while keeping enemies within the player's threat area instead of their previous "dash away" behavior

Slash/Stab Penetration and Resistance

Developed a weapon penetration and resistance system that made sword strikes feel lodged and weighty, with directional pull-out resistance and rotational freedom. It also used my centralized player feedback pipeline to trigger coordinated SFX, VFX, haptics, and decals with managed lifecycles.

Iron Man VR

Performed memory and performance profiling on Iron Man VR, tracing assets and code responsible for memory leaks that were eventually crashing the game. Improved framerate and reduced loading hitches by identifying objects that needed to be prewarmed, and resolved more than 50 critical or progression-blocking bugs while working within a large scale co-development partner's production pipeline.

Box and Spring Physics Simulation

Designed a variable box-and-spring system in C++ that physically responds as anchor points move through user input. This is the foundation for dynamic, physics-based bridges or other structures affected by moving endpoints or environmental forces.

Particle Simulation

AI Agent Awareness

A single-propagation hide-and-seek AI agent awareness system in C++ where AI combines terrain analysis with the player's last known position and speed to estimate where the player could currently be. The red cells visualize those potential locations, and the agentic awareness code is designed to be portable across game engines and agent types.

Hi there, my name is Adam Rhoades. I am a Gameplay Engineer based in Renton, Washington, with 4 years of professional game development experience and an additional 5 years of C# software development experience in ERP business software. I worked on and helped ship Marvel’s Iron Man VR along with three other unreleased studio projects. My work so far has included authoring gameplay systems such as player movement, universal feedback (VFX, SFX, haptics, UI, debris, decals, etc), VR interactions, damage systems, combat features, damage systems, rapid prototyping, and performance profiling.

Alongside my professional experience, I hold a Master of Science in Computer Science from DigiPen Institute of Technology, with a concentration in Computer Graphics, as well as a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from Pacific Lutheran University with a minor in Mathematics. My background has given me a strong foundation in C#, C++, maintainable and scalable architecture, and gameplay programming. I am comfortable working within large, unfamiliar codebases, adapting to established workflows, and translating design goals into practical, maintainable gameplay features.

As a developer, I value thoughtful collaboration, clear communication, and careful problem solving. I enjoy working closely with designers, artists, and engineers to build systems that support both the player experience and the broader needs of the project. I am particularly motivated by gameplay challenges that require rapid iteration, strong technical judgment, and an awareness of performance, usability, and long-term maintainability.
